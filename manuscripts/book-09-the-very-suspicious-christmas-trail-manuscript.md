# **Waffles & Pip and the Very Suspicious Christmas Trail**

Snow had fallen quietly through the night.

By morning, the woodland looked completely different.

Every stump wore a white hat.
Every branch sparkled.
And every rabbit footprint looked extremely important.

Waffles stood in the snow and stared.

“Pip.”

Pip landed beside him.

“Yes?”

“Something happened.”

Pip looked around.

“It snowed.”

Waffles nodded slowly.

“Exactly.”

---

Waffles hopped toward the path.

Then stopped.

Something shiny was half buried in the snow.

He dug with both paws.

**JINGLE!**

Waffles jumped backward.

Pip blinked.

It was a little silver bell.

Waffles stared at it.

“Pip.”

“No.”

“You don’t even know what I was going to say.”

“I have experience.”

Waffles held up the bell.

“Christmas is losing pieces.”

Pip sighed.

“That is not how Christmas works.”

---

They followed the snowy trail.

A little farther along, Waffles found a bright red ribbon tangled in a bush.

He pointed dramatically.

“Another piece.”

“It is ribbon.”

“Christmas ribbon.”

“It could be anybody’s ribbon.”

Waffles looked at Pip.

“Do you know many squirrels wearing red ribbon?”

Pip thought about Squirrel.

“…Unfortunately, yes.”

---

They kept going.

Soon they found something else.

Cookie crumbs.

Waffles lowered his nose to the snow.

Pip immediately said, “Do not eat evidence.”

Waffles froze.

“I was inspecting it.”

“With your mouth?”

“It is a very advanced technique.”

---

Mouse popped out from beneath a snowy log.

“What are you doing?”

“Saving Christmas,” said Waffles.

“We are following litter,” said Pip.

Mouse looked at the cookie crumbs.

“Can I help?”

Waffles nodded.

“Yes. But Pip says we cannot eat the evidence.”

Mouse looked disappointed.

“Pip ruins everything.”

“I am standing right here.”

---

Squirrel joined them next.

Then Hazel.

Then Mole appeared from beneath the snow wearing what looked like half a wreath around his neck.

Pip stared.

Mole looked down.

“Oh.”

“Did you find that underground?” Pip asked.

“No.”

A long pause.

“It found me.”

---

The trail continued between the trees.

Bell.

Ribbon.

Cookie crumbs.

A tiny golden ornament.

One green mitten.

Waffles held up the mitten.

“Pip.”

“No.”

“Christmas has lost a hand.”

Pip closed his eyes.

“Christmas does not have hands.”

“That explains why it keeps dropping things.”

---

Behind them came a small voice.

“Did I miss anything?”

Everyone turned.

Tilly was slowly making her way through the snow.

“No,” said Pip.

“Nothing,” said Waffles.

Mouse nodded.

“Absolutely nothing.”

Tilly smiled.

“Good.”

And kept walking.

---

Then they heard it.

**Jingle.**

Everyone stopped.

**Jingle-jingle.**

Waffles’ ears shot straight up.

“Pip.”

“Yes.”

“It’s getting louder.”

“Yes.”

“Christmas knows we’re following it.”

“No.”

---

They hurried toward the sound.

Waffles hurried fastest.

Which was why Waffles was also the first one to disappear into a snowbank.

**POOF!**

Only his ears remained above the snow.

Pip landed on one.

“Are you all right?”

A muffled voice came from below.

“Completely.”

“Do you need help?”

“No.”

Another pause.

“Yes.”

---

Once Waffles had been removed from the snowbank, the group continued.

Then the trees began to thin.

Beyond them was a fence.

Beyond the fence was a barn.

And beyond the barn…

Waffles stopped.

Pip stopped beside him.

They knew this place.

Waffles whispered:

“Pip.”

“Yes.”

“It’s Charlie’s farm.”

---

A familiar pink nose appeared through the fence.

Then two familiar ears.

**OINK!**

“CHARLIE!”

Waffles shot across the snow.

Charlie squealed happily and raced toward them.

The two friends met in a magnificent collision of rabbit, pig, snow, and absolutely no brakes.

Pip landed on the fence.

“I see nobody learned anything from last time.”

---

Charlie sniffed the silver bell in Waffles’ paw.

Then the ribbon.

Then the mitten.

Then Charlie turned toward the farmhouse.

Waffles looked at Pip.

Pip looked at Charlie.

Then at the trail.

Then back at Charlie.

“Oh,” said Pip.

Waffles gasped.

“Charlie knows Christmas!”

Pip rubbed his forehead.

“I was going to say the trail leads to the farm.”

“That too.”

---

Charlie trotted toward the barn.

Everyone followed.

The farm was glowing.

Lights twinkled along the fence.

A wreath hung on the barn door.

Garland curled around the porch.

And beside a tall Christmas tree stood a girl in a warm winter coat…

…and unmistakable **purple boots**.

Waffles froze.

“Pip.”

Pip smiled.

“I see her.”

“The boot girl.”

“Waffles.”

“What?”

“She probably has a name.”

---

Beside her stood Grandma.

And perched proudly on Grandma’s shoulder was a bright macaw.

Waffles’ eyes widened.

“The giant Pip.”

Pip nearly fell off the fence.

“I beg your pardon?”

The macaw tilted his head.

“GIANT PIP!” he squawked.

Waffles grinned.

“I like him.”

“I don’t.”

---

Lily looked toward Charlie.

Then she saw Waffles.

Then Pip.

Her eyes grew wide.

“You!”

Waffles looked behind himself.

“Me?”

“You’re the rabbit!”

Waffles stood taller.

“I am.”

“And the little blue bird!”

Pip puffed his feathers.

“At last, proper recognition.”

“I saw you when Charlie came home!”

Waffles whispered to Pip:

“She remembers us.”

“You are difficult to forget.”

---

Grandma smiled.

“So these are Charlie’s friends.”

Charlie gave a proud little grunt.

Waffles held out the silver bell.

“We brought back Christmas.”

Pip landed beside him.

“We found several things along the trail.”

Waffles nodded.

“Because Christmas was falling apart.”

Grandma looked at Pip.

Pip shook his head very slightly.

Grandma understood immediately.

---

Lily laughed.

“That bell fell off our basket!”

She took the red ribbon.

“And this.”

Then the little ornament.

“And this!”

She looked at the green mitten.

Grandma looked at Lily.

Lily looked down at her hands.

One mitten.

“Oh.”

Waffles whispered:

“I told you Christmas lost a hand.”

Pip whispered back:

“Do not make this worse.”

---

Grandma explained that she and Lily had been carrying decorations from the shed when Charlie bumped the basket.

Charlie looked offended.

Grandma raised an eyebrow.

Charlie looked less offended.

“And apparently,” Grandma continued, “we left a little Christmas trail all the way through the woods.”

Waffles puffed out his chest.

“Which we followed.”

Pip nodded.

“For several miles.”

“Heroically.”

“Hungrily.”

Mouse quietly ate one of the remaining cookie crumbs.

---

Lily picked up a box beside the tree.

Inside were ornaments, bows, little wooden stars, bells, and tiny candy canes.

“We still have decorating to do.”

She looked at Waffles and Pip.

“Would you like to help?”

Waffles gasped.

Pip smiled.

Charlie squealed.

Cracker flapped his wings.

“HELP! HELP! HELP!”

Pip looked at him.

“I continue to have concerns about that bird.”

---

Lily handed Waffles a shiny red ornament.

“Hang this somewhere special.”

Waffles studied the tree carefully.

Then climbed onto a box.

Then another box.

Then Charlie.

“Waffles,” said Pip.

“I almost have it.”

“You are standing on a pig.”

Charlie seemed perfectly happy about this.

“That does not make it better.”

---

Lily chose a sparkling star.

Grandma tied a red scarf around Charlie.

Mouse found the cookie plate.

Squirrel found the nut bowl.

Mole accidentally decorated himself with garland again.

Hazel hung tiny bells along the fence.

Tilly finally reached the tree.

She looked around.

“Did I miss anything?”

Everyone answered together.

“NO!”

Tilly smiled.

“Good.”

---

Pip helped Lily straighten a bow.

She was wearing her purple boots, a warm coat, and a bright Christmas scarf.

Waffles looked at her.

“Those are very good boots.”

Lily looked down proudly.

“They’re my favorite.”

“I knew it,” said Waffles.

Pip blinked.

“You knew what?”

“That they were important boots.”

---

Grandma carried out a tray.

Warm cookies.

Apple slices.

Carrots.

Waffles stared at the carrots.

Then at Grandma.

Then at the carrots.

“You have excellent judgment.”

Grandma laughed.

“I had a feeling you might think so.”

Cracker leaned toward Waffles.

“CARROTS!”

Waffles nodded.

“Finally. Someone here understands me.”

Pip looked deeply offended.

---

As evening settled over the farm, the lights began to glow brighter.

The barn sparkled.

Snow twinkled beneath the moon.

Charlie curled up near the porch.

Lily sat beside Grandma.

Cracker tucked his head beneath one wing.

Waffles and Pip sat together near the Christmas tree.

For once…

Waffles was quiet.

Pip looked at him.

“You all right?”

Waffles nodded.

“I was thinking.”

“That is usually when trouble starts.”

---

Waffles looked at Grandma.

At Lily in her purple boots.

At Charlie.

At their woodland friends gathered around the farm.

Then at the little silver bell now hanging from the tree.

“We thought we were following Christmas.”

Pip smiled.

“I know.”

“But we weren’t.”

“No.”

Waffles thought a little longer.

“We were following everybody.”

Pip looked around.

“I think maybe that’s closer.”

---

Lily walked over and sat beside them.

“I’m glad you found the trail.”

Waffles smiled.

“So am I.”

Then he leaned toward Pip.

“Pip?”

“Yes?”

“If we hadn’t followed the crumbs…”

“Yes?”

“We might never have found the cookies.”

Pip sighed.

“There it is.”

---

Grandma called everyone toward the porch.

“One Christmas picture!”

Everyone crowded together.

Grandma.

Lily.

Charlie.

Cracker.

Mouse.

Squirrel.

Mole.

Hazel.

Tilly.

Waffles.

And Pip.

Cracker spread his wings.

“CHRISTMAS!”

The camera clicked.

And at exactly that moment…

Waffles sneezed.

**ACHOO!**

Snow fell from the porch roof onto everyone.

---

Silence.

Waffles slowly looked around.

“Pip?”

“No.”

“I haven’t even asked yet.”

“No.”

“Is Christmas snow covered by insurance?”

Pip stared at him.

Grandma laughed.

Lily laughed.

Soon everyone was laughing.

Even Pip.

Eventually.

---

That night, Waffles and Pip headed back toward the woodland.

The little farm glowed behind them.

Waffles looked over his shoulder.

“Pip?”

“Yes?”

“Do you think we’ll see them again?”

Pip smiled.

“I think there are a lot of trails around here.”

Waffles nodded.

“Good.”

He hopped forward.

Then stopped.

“Pip.”

“What now?”

Waffles pointed into the snow.

A candy cane.

Pip groaned.

Waffles grinned.

“Another mystery.”

And off they went.

## **The End**

—or, considering Waffles—

## **Probably Not.** 🎄🐇🐦
